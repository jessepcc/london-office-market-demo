# Analyze macro drivers

## Question and evidence

Through what mechanism could a macro change affect London office demand or supply? Specify the proposed path: employer activity to hiring and space needs; financing rates to development feasibility; inflation to operating costs. Prefer relevant Bank of England, ONS or other primary statistical releases and distinguish official data from forecasts and commentary. UK-wide evidence is context unless a London/sector connection is supported.

## Analysis, example and exhibit

Preserve announcement versus effective dates, publication versus reference periods, revisions, seasonal adjustment, nominal versus real measures and geographic coverage. A newly published release about an old quarter is fresh publication with old-period data, not a current economic shock. Compare only compatible series and dates. Do not estimate rental elasticity, causal effects or statistical significance; these are outside the current tools. Rates and rents moving together is insufficient causal evidence.

Test a hiring-led demand hypothesis against sector divergence, remote-work practice and lagged leasing commitments. A concrete example can pair a dated official policy-rate decision with a separately sourced London employer hiring or relocation announcement; label the connection interpretation and avoid claiming the decision caused the move. Use compatible primary macro histories where normalized evidence exists; otherwise cite dated context in text with the London linkage gap. Landlord watchpoints concern occupier sectors, financing-sensitive future supply and lease decisions, rather than valuation or interest-rate predictions. State missing London employment detail, revisions, lag uncertainty and untested mechanisms explicitly.

## Complete the analysis

Query relevant evidence first, including qualitative categories without a metric filter. Inspect totals, next_offset, supporting excerpts and conflicts before inferring absence. Search and retrieve when the evidence is missing or too old for the question. Cache exact excerpts as candidate evidence. This package cannot approve or normalize monitoring feeds.

Test the proposed mechanism against the strongest available counterevidence and identify a dated catalyst or observable follow-up that would change the interpretation. Finish with scoped cited facts, labeled interpretation, publisher forecasts, monitorable watchpoints and material gaps. Check each excerpt against its claim's dates, geography, definition and strength. Unknown dates remain unknown. State the evidence boundary and retained old periods relative to the run date.

Use compare for compatible arithmetic. For a useful exhibit explicitly load choose_visualization, then prepare_visualization_data, and use the prepared chart ID. Missing history warrants a table or text. Follow the shared run budget and finish before exhaustion.

Treat rates, economy and employment as independent required lanes in full-market coverage. A rates/CPI passage does not establish employment, and London GVA does not establish office jobs. Inspect exact reviewed passage facets or metrics. GLA employment commentary has an unspecified survey window; retain qualitative scope. ONS London residence-based labour-market levels concern residents, not London-office jobs or floor-space demand. UK policy is context, not a London-specific measure. Reference the audited claim per lane and its unresolved applicability.

Keep statistics, estimates and forecasts separate. Attribute GLA's weaker-demand explanation as interpretation. For ONS May-July 2026 use separate clauses: employment 73.9% and inactivity 20.5% concern London residents aged 16–64; unemployment 6.8% concerns economically active London residents aged 16+. Its denominator is those in work plus those seeking and available to work. Cite the exact London row and same-document age header, unemployment glossary and residence-definition leaves. These are resident rates, not London office-job counts or demand forecasts.
